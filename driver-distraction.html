Research Problem

Why an isolated distraction score is incomplete

Driver monitoring systems that ignore external road context produce either dangerous under-alerting or continuous alarm fatigue.

Distraction severity depends on driving context

Traditional driver monitoring flags distraction in isolation. Looking away from the road for two seconds on an empty straight rural highway is risky, but glancing away for the exact same duration near an active intersection or in bumper-to-bumper traffic represents an imminent crash hazard.

Isolated binary alerts trigger warning fatigue

Systems that fire high-urgency alarms for every brief glance induce alarm fatigue, leading operators to ignore or disable safety devices. Risk interpretation must scale dynamically with immediate environmental hazards.

Action classification without gaze is blind to attention

A CNN classifying cabin images can mark a posture as 'safe driving' while the driver's eyes are actually diverted downward or toward side mirrors. Gaze direction and eye openness are critical prerequisites to verify visual focus on the road.

Roadway geometry bottlenecks demand proactive alerts

Entering sharp curves, approaching multi-lane intersections, or navigating complex road junctions demand elevated situational alertness. Detecting secondary activities in these topological zones requires escalated, actionable intervention.

The CITOS Research Contribution

Conventional driver monitoring systems isolate secondary cabin activity from external driving reality. Computer vision literature extensively explores cabin activity recognition, but rarely integrates immediate road density and topological hazards into the risk verdict. Furthermore, commercial fleet telematics often log flat violation tallies without considering whether the vehicle was traversing a clear straightway or negotiating a hazardous curve. CITOS bridges cabin vision, eye-gaze tracking, forward vehicle detection, and geospatial OpenStreetMap routing into a context-aware risk engine.

Research Objectives

  • Train and evaluate an EfficientNet-B0 CNN on driver cabin images for fine-grained secondary behaviour classification across 10 distinct classes.
  • Implement an eye and iris tracking pipeline using MediaPipe Face Mesh with neutral calibration and temporal smoothing to detect sustained off-road visual deviation.
  • Integrate forward road awareness via YOLOv8 vehicle detection to categorize surrounding traffic density into discrete operational tiers (Low, Medium, High).
  • Develop a geospatial graph pipeline combining GPS coordinates with OpenStreetMap road network data to detect upcoming curves and intersections with distance lookahead.
  • Formulate a context-aware risk scoring engine that fuses driver behaviour, gaze attention, traffic density, and road hazards into calibrated risk levels (Safe, Low, Medium, High, Critical).
  • Build an interactive real-time dashboard and modular FastAPI service supporting webcam capture, calibration, image analysis, and live safety telemetry.

Research Questions

  1. RQ1RQ1: Does EfficientNet-B0 achieve strong multi-class cabin activity recognition while maintaining parameter efficiency suitable for real-time vehicular pipelines?
  2. RQ2RQ2: Can iris landmark displacement relative to ocular corners reliably differentiate forward gaze from off-road visual diversion after a 30-frame calibration routine?
  3. RQ3RQ3: How effectively does forward traffic density estimation from YOLOv8 modulate the contextual severity of a detected driver distraction?
  4. RQ4RQ4: Can OpenStreetMap road topology coupled with real-time GPS coordinates reliably anticipate sharp curves and intersections ahead of the vehicle?
  5. RQ5RQ5: Does a multi-factor context fusion engine produce situationally proportional warnings without generating excessive false alarms in low-risk environments?

Component 01·Distraction CNN

Driver Distraction Detection

What It Does

Classify driver secondary activities from cabin camera video frames into 10 discrete behavioral categories.

Why It Is Needed

Secondary tasks such as texting, handheld calling, and reaching behind divert the driver's cognitive and physical control from driving. Automated visual classification provides early indication of unsafe driver engagement.

Input Stream

Single driver/cabin video frame (RGB image) cropped or scaled from in-vehicle camera.

Model / Algorithm

EfficientNet-B0 CNN (compound coefficient scaling depth, width, and resolution)

Component Output

Predicted activity class (c0–c9) and classification confidence score (0.00–1.00).

Processing & Methodology Pipeline

  1. 1Frame capture and dimension extraction from cabin video stream
  2. 2Bilinear resizing to standard 224 × 224 × 3 input tensor
  3. 3Pixel normalization using standard ImageNet mean and variance constants
  4. 4Training-time data augmentations: random horizontal flip, rotation (±15°), brightness jitter
  5. 5Forward pass through pretrained EfficientNet-B0 CNN backbone
  6. 6Dense classifier layer with Softmax activation yielding 10-class probability vector

Key Operational Parameters

ParameterValueDescription
Input Resolution224 × 224 × 3Standard EfficientNet-B0 spatial input dimensions
Backbone Parameters~5.3 MillionLightweight parameter count vs >25M for ResNet-50
Classifier Classes10 categoriesc0 (safe) through c9 (passenger interaction)
OptimizerAdam / AdamWAdaptive learning rate with weight decay during fine-tuning

Evaluation & Verification Status

Final Training Accuracy: 99.74%, Final Validation Accuracy: 84.85%, Best Validation Loss: 0.6121. Generalization gap is 14.89%. Independent test metrics are not reported.

Role in the Complete CITOS Platform

Acts as the primary behavioral contributor to the Driver Risk sub-score, establishing whether the driver is in a safe posture or engaged in a secondary task.

Implementation Note: EfficientNet-B0 was selected over larger backbones due to its superior accuracy-to-parameter ratio, making it well-suited for near-real-time server or edge deployment.

Component 02·Gaze & Attention

Driver Attention / Gaze Monitoring

What It Does

Estimate real-time visual attention and line of sight to confirm whether the driver's eyes are actively focused on the forward road.

Why It Is Needed

A driver might maintain hands on the steering wheel while looking away toward side mirrors, infotainment screens, or down at their lap. Direct gaze tracking detects inattention independently of hand position.

Input Stream

Real-time driver facial camera frames.

Model / Algorithm

MediaPipe Face Mesh + Iris Landmark Geometry + Temporal Smoothing Filter

Component Output

Gaze direction state, normalized horizontal/vertical iris displacements, calibration status, and sustained off-road flag.

Processing & Methodology Pipeline

  1. 1Facial detection and 468-point 3D landmark regression via MediaPipe Face Mesh
  2. 2Refined iris landmark extraction (indices 468–477) identifying pupil centers
  3. 3Calculation of horizontal (ear-to-nose) and vertical iris position ratios relative to ocular corners
  4. 4Baseline user calibration routine gathering 30 neutral, forward-looking frames
  5. 5Temporal smoothing over sliding frame history using exponential moving average
  6. 6Gaze state categorization: LOOKING FORWARD, LOOKING LEFT, LOOKING RIGHT, LOOKING DOWN
  7. 7Sustained deviation timer measuring continuous off-road gaze duration

Key Operational Parameters

ParameterValueDescription
Calibration Samples30 framesNeutral baseline frames gathered on startup
Horizontal Threshold±0.15 offsetDisplacement threshold triggering left/right gaze
Vertical Threshold-0.12 offsetDisplacement threshold triggering downward gaze
Temporal Window5–10 framesMoving average filter window to suppress micro-saccades
Sustained Timer≥ 1.5–2.0 sContinuous deviation threshold marking high-risk inattention

Evaluation & Verification Status

Empirical threshold validation; formal 3D head-pose angle ground truth was not recorded in this evaluation phase.

Role in the Complete CITOS Platform

Directly modulates the Driver Risk score: a secondary distraction with eyes forward is treated as lower risk than an off-road glance sustained over 2 seconds.

Implementation Note: Handles missing face detections gracefully with explicit 'NO FACE' and 'NOT CALIBRATED' states, preventing uncalibrated alerts.

Component 03·Traffic Density

Traffic Density Estimation

What It Does

Estimate surrounding vehicle volume and congestion from the road-facing dashcam to quantify immediate traffic risk.

Why It Is Needed

Driver distraction in dense, stop-and-go traffic carries a severe collision hazard due to sudden braking by lead vehicles. Distraction on an open road offers substantially larger margins for error.

Input Stream

Forward road-facing dashcam video frame (RGB).

Model / Algorithm

YOLOv8 (Ultralytics) convolutional object detector

Component Output

Detected vehicle count, bounding box coordinates with confidences, and traffic density tier (Low / Medium / High).

Processing & Methodology Pipeline

  1. 1Ingestion of forward roadway video frames
  2. 2YOLOv8 single-stage convolutional object detection inference
  3. 3Target class filtering restricted to vehicular categories: car, truck, bus, motorcycle
  4. 4Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) to eliminate duplicate candidate bounding boxes
  5. 5Summation of detected vehicle count in vehicle path corridor
  6. 6Categorical bucketing into discrete traffic density tiers: Low, Medium, High

Key Operational Parameters

ParameterValueDescription

IoU NMS Threshold0.45Intersection-over-Union threshold for box de-duplication
Low Density0–2 vehiclesLight or uncongested road conditions
Medium Density3–6 vehiclesModerate traffic flow requiring standard following distance
High Density7+ vehiclesHeavy congestion with elevated rear-end collision probability

Evaluation & Verification Status

Pretrained and fine-tuned YOLOv8 model; specific mAP metrics on local transit dashcam test sets are not published in this phase.

Role in the Complete CITOS Platform

Feeds directly into the Road Risk calculation, dynamically elevating environmental danger whenever traffic volume rises.

Implementation Note: Vehicle bounding boxes are processed asynchronously to maintain high frame throughput in the pipeline.

Component 04·Route Hazards

Route Hazard Detection

What It Does

Identify upcoming road curvature and intersections ahead of the vehicle by matching real-time GPS coordinates with OpenStreetMap road graph data.

Why It Is Needed

Navigating road bends and junction approaches requires peak situational awareness. Cognitive or visual distraction during curve entry or intersection crossing causes disproportionately severe accidents.

Input Stream

Real-time vehicle GPS coordinates (latitude, longitude, speed, heading) and OpenStreetMap road graph data.

Model / Algorithm

Geospatial road network graph traversal + Bearing deviation + Haversine distance

Component Output

Detected hazard types, distance ahead (in meters), bearing deflection angle, and hazard severity flag.

Processing & Methodology Pipeline

  1. 1GPS coordinate matching to nearest OpenStreetMap highway node / edge
  2. 2Forward trajectory lookahead along the vehicle's road segment (50m to 150m horizon)
  3. 3Bearing angle change computation between successive road nodes for curve detection
  4. 4Node connectivity degree analysis (>2 highway branches) for intersection identification
  5. 5Proximity distance computation using Haversine geodesic distance formulation
  6. 6Hazard categorization: Sharp curve ahead, Moderate curve ahead, Intersection ahead, Intersection very close

Key Operational Parameters

ParameterValueDescription
Lookahead Horizon150 metersSpatial window scanned along upcoming road trajectory
Sharp Curve ThresholdBearing Δ > 40°Direction deflection over a 30m road window
Moderate Curve ThresholdBearing Δ 20°–40°Mild roadway bend requiring steering adjustment
Intersection Critical< 30 metersImmediate intersection approach demanding full road focus
Intersection Warning30–100 metersUpcoming intersection transition zone

Evaluation & Verification Status

Topological verification against OpenStreetMap network maps along regional test corridors; GPS multipath jitter handling remains an active area of refinement.

Role in the Complete CITOS Platform

Provides geometric road context that proactively increases the Road Risk score prior to physical entry into high-risk road features.

Implementation Note: Caching local OSM graph subgraphs prevents latency spikes when vehicles travel across map tile boundaries.

Component 05·Risk Engine

Context-Aware Risk Assessment

What It Does

Synthesize driver behaviour, gaze attention, traffic density, and road hazards into a unified, situation-aware driving risk index (0–100) and actionable warning.

Why It Is Needed

Distraction must never be judged in a vacuum. Combining cabin telemetry with external environmental danger yields proportional, meaningful warnings while eliminating false alarms on clear roads.

Input Stream

Driver distraction class & confidence, gaze direction state & sustained timer, traffic density tier, and route hazard list with distances.

Model / Algorithm

Multi-Factor Context Fusion Engine with Asymmetric Escalation Logic

Component Output

Real-time composite Risk Score (0–100), categorical Risk Level, Driver Sub-Score, Road Sub-Score, and Actionable Warning message.

Processing & Methodology Pipeline

  1. 1Compute Driver Risk Sub-Score (0–100): Weighted sum of activity severity class (c0 = 0 to c1/c3 = 85) multiplied by gaze state modifier (sustained off-road gaze escalates score)
  2. 2Compute Road Risk Sub-Score (0–100): Base traffic density score (Low = 10, Medium = 35, High = 65) + Sharp curve bonus (+25) + Intersection proximity bonus (+25)
  3. 3Context Fusion: Non-linear interaction between Driver Risk and Road Risk — high driver risk paired with high road risk triggers super-linear escalation
  4. 4Calibration into discrete Risk Tiers: Safe (0–19), Low (20–39), Medium (40–59), High (60–79), Critical (80–100)
  5. 5Generate prioritized actionable text warning targeted directly at the immediate hazard

Key Operational Parameters

ParameterValueDescription
Safe Tier0–19Normal driving, eyes on forward road
Low Tier20–39Minor distraction on clear, straight road
Medium Tier40–59Distraction with moderate traffic OR curve ahead
High Tier60–79Severe distraction (texting/phone) with traffic or nearby hazard
Critical Tier80–100Sustained off-road gaze or severe distraction approaching hazard in traffic

Evaluation & Verification Status

Validated across diverse scripted test scenarios demonstrating dynamic alert differentiation. Formal closed-loop simulator safety studies are marked as future work.

Role in the Complete CITOS Platform

The central intelligence core of the distraction subsystem, bridging raw multi-stream sensing with driver feedback and fleet telemetry.

Implementation Note: Implemented as a modular pure Python evaluation service with sub-millisecond calculation overhead once inputs are parsed.

Engineering Architecture

Implementation and engineering details

Detailed specifications of the data pipelines, mathematical transforms, calibration routines, and microservice APIs.

Architecture Spec

Model Architecture & Preprocessing

EfficientNet-B0 employs compound coefficient scaling to balance network depth, width, and resolution. Input frames are standardized to 224 × 224 × 3 with bilinear interpolation and normalized using ImageNet channel statistics. Training incorporates random horizontal flips, rotation (±15°), and brightness jitter to prevent overfitting on the State Farm dataset.

Architecture Spec

Gaze Calibration & Saccade Filtering

Because eye morphology and seating position differ between individuals, the system collects a 30-frame neutral baseline upon session startup. Normalized iris displacement is computed relative to the outer and inner canthi. An exponential moving average filter smooths transient saccades, firing an off-road event only when deviation persists beyond 1.5 seconds.

Architecture Spec

Traffic Density Corridor Filtering

YOLOv8 inference processes forward dashcam imagery. Detections are restricted to vehicle classes (car, truck, bus, motorcycle) with confidence ≥ 0.35. A spatial corridor filter isolates vehicles in the active travel lane, bucketing counts into discrete tiers (Low: 0–2, Medium: 3–6, High: 7+) to represent situational density.

Architecture Spec

Geospatial Road Graph Lookahead

Real-time GPS fixes are snapped to OpenStreetMap graph edges. Lookahead algorithms traverse the downstream path up to 150 meters, calculating bearing delta angles between consecutive nodes. Nodes with degree > 2 are flagged as intersections, computing exact Euclidean and geodesic proximity to the vehicle.

Architecture Spec

Context Fusion & Risk Scoring Logic

Driver risk (distraction severity × gaze status) and road risk (traffic density + curvature + intersection proximity) are fused through an asymmetric non-linear function. If both driver risk and road risk are elevated, a multiplicative penalty triggers instant escalation to High or Critical tiers. When driving is safe (c0), high road risk yields an informational advisory rather than a distraction violation.

Architecture Spec

Asynchronous API & Frontend Pipeline

FastAPI handles frame ingestion asynchronously, preventing blocking during model execution. The React frontend interacts via REST endpoints, offering instant visual feedback on radial gauges, driver status indicators, and hazard countdown cards with built-in client fallback simulation.

Scientific Rigor

Project Limitations

In keeping with academic research ethics, we explicitly document every empirical limitation and constraint discovered during development.

1

Training vs. Validation Generalization Gap

The EfficientNet-B0 model achieved 99.74% training accuracy and 84.85% validation accuracy on the State Farm dataset — a 14.89% gap. This indicates noticeable overfitting to training subjects and camera setups. In real commercial buses, varying camera mounts, steering wheel occlusions, and diverse driver physiognomy can broaden this gap.

2

Held-Out Test Set Metrics Not Reported

Independent held-out test split metrics (such as test accuracy, mAP, precision, recall, and F1 scores on unseen drivers) were not published in the project repository. True zero-shot cross-driver generalizability remains unverified without a formal leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) study.

3

Empirical Gaze Calibration Thresholds

MediaPipe Face Mesh gaze tracking relies on empirical threshold offsets (±0.15 horizontal, -0.12 vertical) relative to a 30-frame neutral baseline. The current implementation does not incorporate calibrated 3D pupil corneal reflection ground truth or calibrated head-pose sensors.

4

Geospatial & GPS Network Dependencies

Route hazard detection depends on continuous GPS lock and comprehensive OpenStreetMap graph coverage. Satellite multipath interference in dense urban canyons, bridge underpasses, and unmapped rural roads can degrade lookahead precision.

5

In-Cabin Environmental Sensitivities

Low-light nighttime driving, severe direct solar glare, polarized sunglasses, and physical camera obstructions represent uncontrolled real-world failure modes that were not comprehensively evaluated under closed laboratory datasets.

Research Roadmap

Future Work and Extensions

Active directions for refining model generalizability, hardware acceleration, and longitudinal fleet safety integration.

Phase 1

Validation on Naturalistic In-the-Wild Datasets

Evaluate the model against in-the-wild, naturalistic driver monitoring benchmarks such as Drive&Act, DMD (Driver Monitoring Dataset), or D-DD (Driver Distraction Dataset) using strict leave-one-subject-out cross-validation.

Phase 2

Calibrated 3D Head-Pose & Eye-Gaze Sensor Fusion

Integrate infrared corneal reflection cameras and calibrate true 3D gaze angles in degrees against fixed cabin zones (windshield, left mirror, right mirror, dashboard cluster, center console).

Phase 3

Temporal Sequence Modeling Over Video Windows

Transition from single-frame CNN classification to temporal architectures (such as BiLSTM, 3D ConvNets, or Video Vision Transformers) over sliding 30–60 frame windows to accurately capture activity initiation, duration, and termination.

Phase 4

Hardware Edge Deployment & Latency Optimization

Quantize the models using TensorRT / ONNX Runtime for edge execution on vehicle-mounted hardware (such as NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano or Hailo-8 AI accelerators), recording verified end-to-end latency and frame-rate figures.

Phase 5

Multi-Modal Warning Actuation in Fleet Environments

Pilot contextual alert modalities inside real passenger buses, including directional seat haptics, heads-up display symbols, and localized voice prompts in Sinhala, Tamil, and English.

Phase 6

Integration with CITOS Fleet Knowledge Graph

Stream context-aware risk scores into the CITOS Neo4j knowledge graph, enabling longitudinal driver coaching and exposure-adjusted driver assessment.
